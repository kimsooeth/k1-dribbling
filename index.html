Overview

The dribbling task is decomposed into three progressively harder subskills. At each stage a new reward term is introduced and earlier terms are de-weighted, so the policy builds each new capability on top of what it already learned instead of solving the full problem in one shot.

StageGoalRunInitialized from
1Approach the ball and keep it in viewB008scratch
2Dribble the ball toward the commanded directionB009B008
3Dribble at commanded direction and commanded speed with a straight trajectoryB011B009

Across all three stages the AMP discriminator contributes 60% of the total reward (disc_reward_weight: 0.6) and the task reward contributes 40% (task_reward_weight: 0.4). The discriminator keeps the motion natural (forward locomotion from the reference dataset), while the task reward shapes the dribbling behavior.

Observation space

The policy observes an 84-dimensional vector: a 50-dim proprioceptive base observation concatenated with a 34-dim task observation. The layout is identical across all three stages.

Base observation (50 dims) — proprioception

Computed in compute_base_obs.

#FieldDimDescription
1gravity_body3Gravity unit vector in the root body frame; tells the policy which way is "down" relative to the torso, a standard proxy for root tilt.
2ang_vel_body3Root angular velocity (roll/pitch/yaw rates) in the root body frame.
3dof_pos22Joint angles for all 22 actuated DoFs of the K1.
4dof_vel22Joint velocities for all 22 actuated DoFs.

All base terms are root-relative (body frame for gravity and angular velocity), which makes the policy invariant to world-frame orientation.

Task observation (34 dims) — dribbling context

Computed in compute_dribbling_observations.

#FieldDimDescription
5ball_pos_history30Last 15 frames of 2D ball position (xy) in the robot's heading frame. In-view frames store the real position; out-of-view frames write zero, so the history doubles as a memory of when the ball was last seen. Flattened as 15 × 2.
6local_tar_dir2Commanded dribbling direction (unit xy) rotated into the heading frame.
7tar_speed1Commanded ball speed (m/s), sampled in [tar_speed_min, tar_speed_max] = [0.5, 2.5].
8in_view11 if the ball is inside the head-camera FOV, else 0. Same signal used by the active-sensing reward.

Design notes

No direct ball velocity. The policy sees ball positions over time, not velocities, and must infer motion from the 15-step history (≈0.5 s at 30 Hz control).

Camera-occlusion memory. Writing zeros when the ball leaves the FOV turns the history into a "when did I last see it" signal rather than a stale estimate. Together with in_view, the policy can distinguish "the ball is right here" from "the ball hasn't been visible for a while".

Stage 1: Approach the ball (B008)

Objective. Walk toward the ball and keep the head tilted so the ball stays within the onboard camera's field of view. No dribbling direction or speed is enforced yet; the only requirement is to close distance.

episode_length: 20.0
reward_chasing_w: 2.0
reward_chasing_scale: 2.0
reward_ball_movement_w: 2.5
ball_movement_min_vel: 0.5
ball_movement_speed_cap: 1.5
reward_ball_dir_w: 0.0     # disabled — direction not enforced yet
reward_ball_speed_w: 0.0   # disabled — speed not enforced yet
reward_active_sensing_w: 0.2
reward_yaw_alignment_w: 0.2
reward_yaw_no_view_w: -0.2

What these weights encode

  • A strong chasing reward (w=2.0) pulls the robot toward the ball.
  • A ball-movement reward (w=2.5) gives broad credit for any ball motion above 0.5 m/s, regardless of direction. This acts as an exploration signal: the robot can stumble into the ball and be rewarded for nudging it.
  • Active-sensing and yaw-alignment terms keep the ball in the camera FOV.

Result. The robot reliably approaches the ball and tilts its head to keep it in view. This is the base behavior everything downstream builds on.

Stage 2: Directional dribbling (B009)

Objective. Dribble the ball toward the commanded direction. Speed is not yet a constraint.

reward_chasing_w: 0.5           # reduced — approach is already learned
reward_chasing_scale: 0.5
reward_ball_dir_w: 2.5          # NEW — primary learning signal this stage
ball_dir_min_vel: 0.1
reward_ball_movement_w: 0.5     # reduced — broad movement no longer the goal
reward_ball_speed_w: 0.0        # still disabled
reward_active_sensing_w: 0.2
reward_yaw_alignment_w: 0.2
reward_yaw_no_view_w: -0.2

What changed from Stage 1

  • Chasing weight drops 2.0 → 0.5. Approach is already learned; heavy chasing would compete with direction control.
  • A new directional reward at w=2.5 pays out when the ball moves in the commanded direction, independent of speed magnitude.
  • Ball-movement reward drops 2.5 → 0.5: the ball should still move, but directed motion is now preferred over any motion.

Result. The robot positions itself relative to the ball and uses the left or right foot to redirect it when it drifts off-line. The improved head tilt (from Stage 1's camera fix) makes ball-relative positioning much more accurate than in earlier runs. The ball travels in roughly the correct direction, but its speed is whatever the policy happens to produce.

Stage 3: Direction + speed + straight trajectory (B011)

Objective. Dribble at both the commanded direction and the commanded speed, keeping the ball on a straight trajectory even when individual kicks are imperfect.

reward_chasing_w: 0.2            # further reduced
reward_chasing_scale: 0.2
reward_ball_dir_w: 2.0           # maintained
reward_ball_speed_w: 1.5         # NEW — matches commanded speed magnitude
reward_ball_speed_scale: 1.0
reward_ball_perp_vel_w: 1.0      # NEW — penalizes sideways ball drift
ball_perp_vel_scale: 4.0
reward_ball_movement_w: 0.0      # disabled — direction+speed now take over
reward_yaw_no_view_w: -0.5       # stronger penalty — keep turning to find ball

What changed from Stage 2

  • A new ball-speed reward (w=1.5) separately rewards matching the commanded speed. Direction and speed are split into two terms rather than one ball-velocity match, so the policy can earn partial credit on each axis, avoiding the all-or-nothing gradient of a joint velocity match.
  • A new perpendicular-velocity term (w=1.0) targets the component of ball velocity perpendicular to the commanded direction. This addresses drift: even if a single kick is imperfect, the trajectory stays close to a straight line on average.
  • The yaw-no-view penalty strengthens -0.2 → -0.5, pushing harder for the robot to turn and reacquire the ball when it leaves the FOV.
  • Ball movement is disabled; speed and direction rewards replace it.

Result. The robot kicks the ball in approximately the commanded direction at approximately the commanded speed, with less lateral drift than Stage 2. Runs from this stage are the candidates for hardware deployment.

Reward terms

All formulas come from compute_dribbling_reward in mimickit/envs/envs_k1/task_k1_dribbling_env.py. Terms that are zero across B008–B011 are omitted.

1. Active sensing

$$r = w \cdot \mathbb{1}\big[\text{ball in camera FOV}\big]$$

Binary per-step reward: 1 when the ball is inside the onboard camera's horizontal/vertical FOV, 0 otherwise. The FOV is anchored to the Head_2 body with a camera pitch offset of −12.2°.

Purpose. A direct incentive to orient the head so the ball is visible; without it the head drifts freely and the vision pipeline loses the ball.

Weight: 0.2 in all three stages.

2. Chasing

$$r = w \cdot \exp\!\left(-\,\text{scale}\cdot\lVert p_{\text{ball}} - p_{\text{robot}}\rVert^2\right)$$

Gaussian on the 2D distance between ball and robot root; peaks at 1 when the robot is on the ball and decays smoothly with distance.

Purpose. Pulls the robot toward the ball. The distance-only form is intentional: it still fires when the ball is out of view.

  • Stage 1: w=2.0, scale=2.0 — dominant, drives approach.
  • Stage 2: w=0.5, scale=0.5 — reduced, approach already learned.
  • Stage 3: w=0.2, scale=0.2 — minimal, prevents wandering off.

3. Yaw alignment — ball in view

$$r = w \cdot \exp\!\left(-\,\text{scale}\cdot\big(\theta_{\text{cmd}}^2 + \theta_{\text{ball}}^2\big)\right)$$

Active only when the ball is in the FOV. \(\theta_{\text{cmd}}\) is the angle between the robot heading and the commanded facing direction; \(\theta_{\text{ball}}\) is the angle between the heading and the direction to the ball.

Purpose. Rewards simultaneously facing the commanded direction and the ball, so the torso lines up before a kick.

Weight: w=0.2, scale=2.0 in all three stages.

4. Yaw alignment — ball out of view (penalty)

$$r = w_{\text{no\_view}} \cdot \Big\lvert\, \dot\psi_{\text{robot}} - \dot\psi_{\max}\cdot \operatorname{sign}\!\big(f_{\text{robot}} \times f_{\text{ball}}\big) \Big\rvert$$

Active only when the ball is not in the FOV. \(\dot\psi_{\text{robot}}\) is the robot's yaw rate and \(\dot\psi_{\max}\) a desired turn rate (1 rad/s). The sign of the cross product tells the robot which way to rotate to bring the ball back into view.

Purpose. With \(w_{\text{no\_view}} < 0\), the robot loses reward unless it turns toward the ball at roughly the desired rate. Replaces the in-view term whenever the ball slips out of the FOV.

  • Stages 1 & 2: w=-0.2 (mild).
  • Stage 3: w=-0.5 (stronger, to fight a collapse-to-stand behavior observed in training).

5. Ball direction

$$r = w \cdot \max\!\big(0,\ \hat v_{\text{ball}}\cdot \hat d_{\text{cmd}}\big)\cdot \mathbb{1}\big[\lVert v_{\text{ball}}\rVert > v_{\min}\big]$$

Dot product of the ball's unit velocity with the commanded unit direction, clamped to [0, 1] and gated on the ball moving faster than ball_dir_min_vel = 0.1 m/s.

Purpose. Rewards direction only, independent of speed. The speed gate prevents farming the reward while the ball sits still, where the normalized direction \(\hat v_{\text{ball}}\) becomes numerically ill-defined as \(v_{\text{ball}}\to 0\).

  • Stage 1: w=0.0 (disabled).
  • Stage 2: w=2.5.
  • Stage 3: w=2.0.

6. Ball speed

$$r = w \cdot \exp\!\left(-\,\text{scale}\cdot\big(\lVert v_{\text{ball}}\rVert - v_{\text{cmd}}\big)^2\right)$$

Gaussian on the error between ball speed and commanded speed; peaks at a match and falls off if the ball is too fast or too slow.

Purpose. Rewards speed only, independent of direction. Introduced in Stage 3 because Stage 2's direction reward does not distinguish 0.3 m/s from 3 m/s. A separate speed term gives independent partial credit, which is easier to optimize than a joint velocity match.

  • Stages 1 & 2: w=0.0 (disabled).
  • Stage 3: w=1.5, scale=1.0.

7. Ball movement

$$r = w \cdot \operatorname{clamp}\!\left(\frac{\lVert v_{\text{ball}}\rVert - v_{\min}}{v_{\text{cap}} - v_{\min}},\ 0,\ 1\right)$$

With ball_movement_max_vel = 0 (no upper ramp-down): a linear ramp from ball_movement_min_vel = 0.5 m/s to ball_movement_speed_cap = 1.5 m/s. Rewards any ball motion regardless of direction, saturating at 1 above the cap.

Purpose. Exploration reward that encourages early contact with the ball, before caring where it goes.

  • Stage 1: w=2.5 (dominant exploration signal).
  • Stage 2: w=0.5 (reduced but active).
  • Stage 3: w=0.0 (disabled; replaced by direction + speed).

8. Ball perpendicular velocity

$$r = w \cdot \mathbb{1}\big[\lVert v_{\text{ball}}\rVert > v_{\min}\big]\cdot \exp\!\left(-\,\text{scale}\cdot v_\perp^2\right)$$

With \(\hat d = (d_x, d_y)\), the perpendicular unit vector is \((-d_y,\, d_x)\) and \(v_\perp = v_{\text{ball}}\cdot(-d_y,\, d_x)\).

Purpose. Discourages sideways (off-axis) ball motion, anchoring the trajectory near the commanded line. Unlike an anchored-line reward it is stateless and per-step: it does not correct existing drift, but stops new drift from accumulating. The speed gate (\(\lVert v_{\text{ball}}\rVert > 0.1\) m/s) prevents reward farming when the ball is stationary.

  • Stages 1 & 2: w=0.0 (disabled).
  • Stage 3: w=1.0, scale=4.0.
